Artsmark assessment

Your Artsmark assessment

The Artsmark award is based on an assessment of your school's current arts and cultural provision against the Artsmark criteria and quality principles.

It is both descriptive and interpretive, meaning rather than a complete record of everything your school has done, it should help your assessor understand what arts and culture look like in your setting, why they matter and what you know about their effect.

Part 1

Practice across the Artsmark criteria

Four areas bring related criteria together and ask you to describe current practice, what enables it, and what you understand about its effect.

Part 2

School voice and distinctiveness

Step back from the individual criteria and tell us what is distinctive, what you value, and what you most want the assessor to understand.

Practice across the Artsmark criteria

How to approach this section

The sections below organise the Artsmark criteria into four related areas. The order is intended to help you move from the arts and cultural experiences pupils have, towards the wider conditions and relationships that support them.

Assessors are interested in an honest account of your current provision, including what is well established, what is emerging and what may be constrained by your context.

For each section, tell us what this looks like in your school now. Choose examples that best represent your current practice. Where possible, go beyond describing activity to help us understand why it matters, who experiences or shapes it, what makes it possible or helps it continue, what you have noticed or learned about its effect, and what helps you know this.

If something is less relevant or less developed in your setting

You can still tell us about your current relationship to it. For example, you might explain:

  • why it is less relevant in your context
  • practical or geographical barriers that affect what is possible
  • what you currently do at a scale that makes sense for your school
  • something you are beginning to explore or understand
  • what an equivalent looks like in your particular setting

Schools are all different. Their size, phase, location, pupils, staffing, resources and community will all affect what arts and cultural provision can and should look like.

This means that not every section or prompt will be equally relevant to every school. They are there to help you consider what might be useful to tell us, not as a checklist of things you are expected to have.

The suggested response is 100–300 words per area, however this will be finalised following the pilot.

      How to approach this section

      While the sections above help us consider your provision against the Artsmark criteria, this part gives you more space to tell us what is particular about your school: what is unique, what you value and what you most want the assessor to understand.

      You might speak about

      • something you are especially proud of achieving
      • where children and young people experience joy, creativity or freedom
      • your contribution to the wider school community
      • an area you have worked hard to strengthen

      You do not need to repeat examples already given unless they help tell a bigger story.

        What you can share

        Please provide one link to a folder containing a small selection of material.

        For the pilot, we suggest a maximum of three items in total. These might include:

        • photographs
        • examples of pupil work
        • a short document or other artefact
        • pupil, family, community or partner contributions
        • links to audio or video

        Please keep any audio or video material to no more than two minutes in total.

        Choose material that adds something difficult to communicate in writing rather than repeating or documenting everything in your application.

        A sentence or two is enough.

        Sharing safely

        Your school is responsible for ensuring that anything shared:

        • follows your own safeguarding, consent and data-protection policies
        • is appropriate to share outside the school
        • has the necessary permissions where children, young people or others can be identified
        • can be accessed by the assessor for the assessment period

        Material may also be the kind of work you would be happy to celebrate or share with other Artsmark schools.

        Please check that:

        • your school profile is up to date
        • your responses describe your current provision
        • you have chosen examples that help the assessor understand your practice rather than trying to record everything
        • where you make claims about impact or change, you have explained what has helped you reach that understanding
        • your application reflects your actual setting, including areas that may be less developed or work differently from other schools
        • any supplementary material meets your school's safeguarding, consent and data-protection requirements

        Please remember you are not writing towards a particular award level. Your assessor will consider the application as a whole against the Artsmark framework and use professional judgement to determine the award.
