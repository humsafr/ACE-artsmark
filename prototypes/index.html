Why we are testing

Help shape a holistic and meaningful Artsmark assessment

We are exploring three possible ways for schools to describe their arts and cultural provision.

The content is still draft and will change. These prototypes give us something tangible to respond to, rather than asking everyone to begin from scratch.

The aim is not to select one “best” option. We want to understand which formats and elements are useful, and what could be combined into a process worth testing further.

Your feedback will help inform what to test with schools so we can understand what creates ease, confidence, clarity or confusion.

About these prototypes

All text is draft and do not represent the final Artsmark assessment.

Nothing is submitted or stored remotely. The wording, questions and interactions are all open to change.

Shared across all three options

Check and update your school profile

Every application might begin with a short profile of the school or setting to give an assessor context before reviewing the application.

Returning schools can check and update their existing profile rather than completing it again from scratch.

Step 2

Assessment application

Three possible starting points

The three options below explore different ways schools could describe their provision, evidence and learning.

Option 1

Framework application

Long-form question under each of the eight Artsmark Framework criteria, with a final narrative question and optional evidence.

Offers
Clear and thorough coverage of the whole framework.
May limit
Can feel long, redundant or fragmented.
Explore Option 1 →

Option 2

Narrative application

Eight long-form questions grouped into four broader sections, with optional evidence and spoken statement.

Offers
A more connected narrative with alternative ways to present information.
May limit
Criteria might overlap and additional media require constraints.
Explore Option 2 →

Option 3

Snapshot application

A structured self-assessment across all eight criteria, followed by narrative examples.

Offers
A more interactive view of the framework and clear link to strengthening.
May limit
Schools may read an implied award level too soon.

Step 3

After assessment

Receive clear, actionable feedback

The assessment should give schools more than an award outcome. Feedback could help them understand where their provision is strongest, what informed the assessor’s view and what they could build on next.

  • An assessed profile across the eight criteria
  • Specific strengths and the evidence behind them
  • Areas where practice is less established or consistent
  • A small number of realistic development priorities
