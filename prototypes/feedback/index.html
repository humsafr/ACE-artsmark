Feedback that helps a school understand what to build on

This example shows how assessment feedback could combine a consistent framework profile with specific observations and practical next steps.

This is an illustrative example.

The profile and comments below are not linked to a real school or assessment decision.

Assessment outcome

Gold Artsmark Award

The school demonstrates a strong and increasingly embedded approach to arts and culture, with clear leadership, broad provision and positive outcomes for children and young people.

Example outcome

Assessed profile

How established practice appears across the framework

This profile shows the relative shape of the assessor’s judgement. It is not an automatic award calculation.

The shape gives an overview. The written feedback explains the evidence, judgement and suggested next steps.

At a glance

Overall assessment

Strongest areas
Values and Ethos, Leadership, Curriculum Design and Delivery
Areas to build on
Children and Young People, Cultural Collaborations
Next review focus
Pupil decision-making, partnership depth and consistency across year groups

What is working well

Recognised strengths

Leadership is becoming more widely shared

The cross-school working group, senior leadership oversight and regular governor review show that responsibility no longer sits only with the Artsmark lead.

Evidence considered

Improvement planning, meeting records and examples from several subject leads.

Arts and culture are visible in everyday curriculum practice

Curriculum planning shows a clearer entitlement and progression across year groups, rather than provision depending mainly on occasional events.

Evidence considered

Curriculum plans, pupil work and staff reflections from different phases.

The school has made purposeful use of its context

Local cultural partnerships have broadened pupils’ experiences and helped staff develop confidence in areas where specialist capacity was limited.

Evidence considered

Partnership examples, staff development activity and pupil participation.

What you could strengthen

Areas to build on

Move from pupil consultation towards pupil influence

Children and young people are regularly asked for their views, but there is less evidence that they influence decisions about provision.

A possible next step

Give pupils clear responsibility for shaping one recurring part of the offer and review what changes as a result.

Make partnership work less dependent on individual projects

Current partnerships provide valuable opportunities, but several appear tied to particular funding, events or staff relationships.

A possible next step

Agree shared longer-term aims with one or two key partners and build these into curriculum or staff development planning.

Strengthen consistency across year groups

Strong practice is evident, but the application suggests that depth and progression still vary across parts of the school.

A possible next step

Use existing strong practice to define a small number of common expectations and review how consistently they are experienced.

Suggested next priorities

Focus for the next stage

These priorities are intended to support planning rather than add a new set of requirements.

  1. 1 Strengthen meaningful pupil decision-making.
  2. 2 Build greater continuity into cultural partnerships.
  3. 3 Make curriculum entitlement and progression more consistent.

Question for testing

What would the assessment need to capture for feedback like this to be fair, specific and useful?

Consider whether the proposed profile, questions and evidence prompts would give an assessor enough material to explain both the judgement and the school’s next opportunities for development.
