What this assessment is for

This assessment is a snapshot of your school’s current arts and cultural provision. It is designed to build a holistic picture made up of strengths and weaknesses that are unique to each school and that evolve over time.

Assessors are looking for an authentic account, which includes thoughtful reflection on what’s working well, where there are challenges and what has been learned.

The assessment is designed to help Artsmark understand:

  • what your school is doing now
  • what difference it is making
  • how you know
  • how embedded and consistent the provision is
  • what is distinctive about arts and culture in your setting

How to approach the form

There’s no need to write polished essays. Short, specific answers are often more useful than broad statements. Give concrete examples, say who was involved or affected, explain what changed, and tell us what information or experience supports your view.

Using evidence

We are interested in what you learned from evidence, not in collecting a large archive of documents. Where the form asks “How do you know?”, tell us what information, feedback or observation you used and what it showed you.

Using AI tools

You may use AI tools to help organise or edit your material, but your school remains responsible for the accuracy and authenticity of the account. Specific examples and findings are more useful than generic or unsupported claims.

The Artsmark framework and quality principles

The Artsmark criteria are the basis for the award decision. They are made visible throughout the assessment, so you can see what assessors are considering and speak directly to what those criteria look like in your school.

Assessors will use professional judgement across the whole application rather than try to score schools precisely against each criterion.

Award outcome

Your Artsmark result

Riverside Community School has been awarded Artsmark Gold because arts and culture are a visible, sustained part of school life, with strong leadership support, a broad curriculum and range of opportunities, and meaningful involvement from children and young people.

Your application showed that this is more than a collection of activities: arts and culture connect to your values, curriculum planning and community relationships, and you were able to explain what difference this makes and how you know.

The strongest evidence was in Values and Ethos, Leadership, Curriculum Design and Delivery, and Range of Offer. The overall picture is strong and embedded, while some areas — particularly systematic CPD, the consistency of cultural partnerships, and how participation is reviewed across different groups — are less developed. These do not detract from the strength of the overall provision, but they give you clear areas to build on during the life of your award.

At a glance

Strongest areas

  • Arts and culture are clearly linked to school values and priorities.
  • Curriculum and enrichment provide broad, coherent opportunities.
  • Children and young people have visible opportunities to influence and lead.

Areas to strengthen

  • Use participation and voice more systematically to identify inequities.
  • Build a more planned approach to staff development across arts subjects.
  • Deepen a small number of cultural partnerships so their impact is sustained.

School highlights

Your school profile

Your criterion responses showed the individual parts of your provision. Your open responses helped the assessor understand how those parts connect and what gives arts and culture their particular character at Riverside.

A creative identity that is visible beyond individual subjects

A consistent thread through your application is that creativity is treated as part of how Riverside understands itself, rather than as an add-on. You linked arts activity to the school improvement plan, described regular opportunities for children to make and perform, and showed how staff use creative approaches across different areas of learning.

Joy, confidence and belonging are part of the value

The application was strongest when it described what children and young people actually experience. Your examples of the lunchtime making studio, pupil-curated exhibition and end-of-year performance gave a concrete sense of enjoyment, pride and ownership.

Your community context shapes the offer

You were clear that many families have limited access to cultural venues outside school. That makes the school’s role as a connector especially important.

Examples from your application

What made your account convincing

  • Your school improvement plan names creativity and cultural entitlement as a curriculum priority, with termly review by senior leaders.
  • Pupil voice led to the introduction of an open-access lunchtime making studio.
  • A progression review in music identified uneven confidence in Years 3 and 4, leading to targeted staff training.
  • The partnership with the local theatre moved from a one-off visit to a sequence of workshops, performance and teacher development.

Looking ahead

What to strengthen next

You said you want to make pupil-led creative activity more consistent across the school, build staff confidence beyond specialist subjects, and make better use of local cultural relationships.

Use the feedback as is most useful for your school

You do not need to turn every development point into an action plan. Your award recognises the quality of your current provision. During the life of the award, you can choose what to sustain, deepen or change in ways that fit your context and priorities.
