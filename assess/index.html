New assessment structure

Tell us about your school, then tell us what arts and culture look like there now.

This assessment is a snapshot of your school's current arts and cultural provision. It is not about proving that everything is perfect, or reconstructing a two-year journey.

What this assessment is for

We want to understand:

  • what your school is doing now
  • what difference it is making
  • how you know
  • what is distinctive about arts and culture in your setting

How to approach it

1. Your school profile

Give us the context we need to understand your setting and current provision. This information is not scored.

2. Your assessment

Use specific examples to show what is happening, what impact it is having and what you have learned from it. There is also space to tell us what matters most about arts and culture in your school.

You do not need to write polished essays. Short, specific answers are often more useful than broad statements.

What helps assessors

Assessors are looking for a clear and credible picture of practice in your setting. Useful answers tend to:

  • give concrete examples
  • explain who was involved or affected
  • say what changed
  • acknowledge where practice is uneven or still developing
  • explain what information, feedback or observation supports your view

You do not need to upload large amounts of evidence. Where relevant, tell us what you looked at and what it showed you.

A note on AI

You can use AI tools to help organise or edit your material, but the account should remain specific to your school. Generic or sweeping language makes it harder for assessors to understand what is actually happening.

What this prototype is testing

Can a clearer, more structured assessment make expectations easier to understand without losing reflection, school voice and distinctiveness?

    Framework criteria in this section
    A · Concrete example

    Cover what happened, who was involved or affected, how widely it applies, what changed and anything that affected the result. Bullet points are welcome.

    B · How do you know?

    What did you use to understand what difference this made?

    Select any sources you used. You do not need to upload them here.

    C · Reflect

    Include where practice is less consistent, fragile or still developing if relevant.

    What makes a useful answer?

    Be specific. Tell us what happened, who experienced it, what changed and how you know. Generic or sweeping claims are harder to assess, even when the writing sounds polished.

    Part 2B

    School voice and distinctiveness

    This part is deliberately more open.

    It should add meaning, identity and coherence rather than repeat the criterion-linked answers.

    What makes arts and culture distinctive in your school?

    This is your space to tell the assessor what matters most about arts and culture in your setting.

    Question 1

    What is distinctive about arts and culture in your school?

    Tell us what matters most about the experience you are trying to create for pupils and why. You might include joy, creativity, expression or freedom.

    Question 2

    What are you proudest of right now?

    This might be something pupils experience, something that has changed, or something your school contributes to families, other schools, cultural organisations or the wider community.

    Using AI tools

    You can use tools to organise or edit your material, but the account remains your school’s responsibility. Generic language, unsupported claims or wording that removes the specifics of your setting will make the application harder to assess.
